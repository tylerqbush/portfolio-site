# Voice Guide — Tyler Quackenbush Portfolio

Source: Ruben Hassid, "Can you detect AI?" (How to AI newsletter, Jul 22 2026). Rules distilled for use on every page of this site. Read this before writing or rewriting any copy.

## The two golden rules

1. **Vary the rhythm.** AI writing sits in a narrow band — mostly 15–20 word sentences, evenly sized paragraphs. Real writing swings: a 5-word sentence next to a 30-word one, one paragraph that's a single line. Force the spread on purpose.
2. **Be concrete, and commit.** Name the thing, the number, the person. "A client" is an AI tell; "Novant Health" isn't. Don't hedge both sides of a claim — pick one and say it.

## Never do this

- Leaked scaffolding: "Certainly! Here's...", "I hope this helps"
- Self-reference: "as an AI", knowledge-cutoff disclaimers
- Placeholder text left in: "[insert example]"
- Hallucinated-looking citations or stats
- "Best regards" sign-offs outside actual emails
- Signposted conclusions: "In conclusion," "Overall," + restatement + uplift — delete, end on the last concrete point instead
- Pep-talk endings: "As we move forward, embracing X will be key" — delete on sight
- Prompt echo: "This page will cover..."

## Banned words — tier 1 (never)

**Verbs:** delve, leverage, underscore, harness, foster, navigate (figurative), utilize, facilitate, streamline, bolster, illuminate, showcase, embark, elevate, empower, unleash, unlock (figurative), uncover, optimize, garner, resonate, revolutionize, shed light on, synthesize, elucidate, transcend, reimagine, intertwine, entwine, grapple with, espouse, exemplify, underpin

**Nouns:** tapestry, landscape (figurative), realm, ecosystem (figurative), paradigm, synergy, testament, beacon, journey (figurative), interplay, intricacies, symphony (figurative), kaleidoscope, tempest, whimsy, quest (figurative), roadmap (figurative), endeavor, myriad, plethora, advancements, trajectory (figurative)

**Adjectives/adverbs:** pivotal, crucial, seamless(ly), robust, vibrant, intricate, meticulous(ly), nuanced, cutting-edge, transformative, game-changing, groundbreaking, unparalleled, invaluable, multifaceted, commendable, indelible, poignant, profound(ly), relentless(ly), tireless(ly), unwavering, unyielding, timeless, ever-evolving, fast-paced

**Stock phrases:** in today's fast-paced world/landscape, it's important/worth noting, plays a pivotal/crucial role in, stands as a testament to, rich tapestry of, navigate the complexities of, in conclusion, in summary, at its core, that being said, a key takeaway, paving the way for, valuable insights, deeper understanding of, shed light on, when it comes to, not only...but also, here's the kicker, dive/deep-dive into, let's explore/unpack, furthermore, moreover, additionally (sentence-initial)

## Banned words — tier 2 (allowed alone, never stacked)

comprehensive, significant(ly), essential, critical, key (adjective), dynamic, innovative, powerful, notable/notably, vital, vast, rich (figurative), deep/deeper (figurative), explore, enhance, ensure, foster, highlight, reveal, engage, embrace (figurative), insights, perspective, framework, approach, strategy, challenges, opportunities, potential, impact(ful), quietly/quiet (figurative), genuinely, truly, remarkably, arguably, thought-provoking, resilience, dedication, high-quality, step-by-step, sustainable/sustainability

## Structure & rhythm

- **Low burstiness is a tell.** Force a spread: at least one ≤6-word sentence and one 25+-word sentence per page. One single-line paragraph, max.
- **Fractal summaries** (previews and recaps at every level — "in this section we'll..." / "as we've seen...") — delete.
- **Listicle in a trenchcoat** ("The first reason is... The second reason is...") — merge into flowing sentences.
- **Uniform staccato** ("X is A. X is B. X is C.") — combine, or vary the frame.

## Punctuation & formatting

- **Em dashes** — the single most obvious tell. AI pieces run 20+; humans run 2–3. Target ≤1 per page section.
- **Bold-first bullets** ("Security: ...") — avoid unless the list genuinely needs scanning.
- **Emoji bullets** — never on this site.
- **Title Case Headings** or colon-split titles ("The Power of X: Why Y Works") — use sentence case.
- **Oxford comma 100% of the time** — drop it occasionally in casual copy (nav labels, microcopy), keep it in case-study prose.

## Content & voice

- **No concrete imagery** — if the first few sentences of a section evoke nothing visible, add a thing, place, number, or name.
- **Proper-noun avoidance** — never "a client," "a tool," "a hospital system." Name it: Novant Health, Agile Defense, Pangram.
- **Uniform positivity** is a tell. Let something be annoying, unresolved, or a genuine tradeoff — this site is allowed to have opinions ("that's a trust problem, not just a design one" is the target register).
- **Both-sidesing every claim** — commit to a position instead.
- **Suspiciously tidy anecdotes** — real case studies have a tangent or a dead end. Keep one.
- **Register scrubbing** — use contractions. Don't sand the voice flat.

## Human markers to add on purpose

Contractions · a number with texture ($43, 11 months, 4:30am) · a named thing (brand, tool, street, person) · a parenthetical aside with attitude · "I think" / "honestly" used once, not per paragraph · a sentence starting with And, But, or Because · one single-sentence paragraph · a mild complaint or unresolved edge · an irrelevant-but-true detail in an anecdote · a dropped Oxford comma in casual copy · a question the reader was genuinely asking · uneven list items (a plain "is" where the draft would reach for "serves as")

## What NOT to overdo when fixing AI-sounding copy

- Don't swap every flagged word for something weird — it becomes its own tell.
- Don't scatter random typos for "authenticity." Errors should read as casualness, not carelessness.
- Don't scrub personality along with the tells — a flat, tell-free page is still AI-shaped.
- Don't invent facts, stats, or quotes to sound more concrete.
- Don't shrink every long sentence. Humans write long sentences too; they just don't write *only* 18-word ones.
