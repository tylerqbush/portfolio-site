01 · The situation

Federal agencies don't sign on a pitch deck.

Every engagement starts the same way: prove the idea works before anyone commits real budget to it, on a clock measured in days, not quarters. Later, the company faced a version of the same problem internally: "make the company AI-native" is a slogan until around 1,500 employees have an actual path to act on it.

02 · The approach

Prove it with a working prototype.

Pre-Discovery is the company's answer to the first problem: a 10-day sprint, stakeholder interviews, and a modified Scrum process that ends in a working concept prototype. Ten days isn't enough time for real end-user access, so we design around proxy users from the start. I helped design that process with my team, workshopping it in Mural, and I help run it. A customer can see and use a working prototype before competitors have written a proposal, and these sprints have helped win federal contracts.

Once a customer signs, a longer Discovery program, 30, 60 or 90 days with real users and data, takes the research further, with workshops, usability testing, surveys and analytics review at each stage.

I've contributed to four of those prototypes, each one a full concept dashboard built in Figma and Mural for a different problem, and each one came with personas and journey maps.

Readiness dashboard

Risk assessment, threat readiness, and training readiness views for a defense client.

DREAMS

A workforce and HR dashboard for federal law enforcement: hiring flow, role assignment, and leadership metrics in place of siloed legacy systems.

GUARD

A municipal infrastructure dashboard that consolidated siloed data sources into one view for faster crisis and emergency response.

COMPLY

A compliance dashboard giving law enforcement leadership a real-time view of assessment activity across field offices and agents.

03 · Leadership and AI strategy

The same discovery-first thinking, at company scale.

Agile Defense University and AI Foundations

The AI problem needed a different kind of prototype: a curriculum. I’m the project manager for Agile Defense University, built on the company's own AI Learning → AI Leaning → AI Leading adoption framework. My team and I draft storyboards, write and validate scripts, then update the storyboards to match before anything moves into production, the same repeatable pattern we're now using to build out the rest of ADU. ADU's goal is for every employee to know exactly where they stand on their career path and to have something they can act on immediately, at whatever speed makes sense for them. The ADU hub is live and part of new-hire orientation, and I helped design the manager feedback form and the Career Baseline Assessment.

The clearest example is AI Foundations, which we released to the whole company in August 2026. It explains AI so anyone can follow it: where it came from, what it is, how to use it well, and what can and can’t go into an AI system depending on someone’s clearance and the classification of the information. Every employee now takes it before using our internal AI tools. I helped storyboard, script, and build it. Make the problem simple, then solve the simple problem: that course is the rule in practice.

Defense data fabric pilot

I project-managed a 16-week, 8-sprint pilot of a defense data fabric ecosystem, starting in April 2025. The team had 13 people (strategists, architects, data engineers, developers and governance advisors) working with five government stakeholders, and I supported discovery along the way. It went live as a working system in 16 weeks. The client asked for more work, and other branches of the military asked for something similar.

Experience Studio and Fast Plays

I’m also the project manager for Experience Studio, the four-person design team behind this work, co-led the Labs project management office for six months during a post-merger reorg (reorgs are never as clean as the org chart makes them look), and helped create Fast Plays. They started as our internal methods wiki and became my contribution to Pre-Discovery’s design: about 20 templated workshop methods that let me set up a discovery workshop in minutes, even before every stakeholder is known, and that any employee can run without deep facilitation experience.

Course video pipeline

The video pipeline for our program management course got the same treatment. Its graphics came from one graphic designer working in Illustrator, one finished module every two weeks, and the team was behind on a strict deadline. I set up a Figma design system in about half a day so assets became drag-and-drop and anyone on the team could jump in. The team went to three modules in three days and ended up two days ahead of schedule. The course is still in production.

Solutions Architect Jira board

Solutions Architects had the same problem in miniature: a multi-phase process tracked in an Excel sheet that one person kept. I designed a Jira board with the lead of the Solutions Architect team that auto-populates each phase's subtasks, linked to the OneDrive folder holding that phase's artifacts and a reference for how to fill each one out. A new solutions architect can join an effort already in progress, at any phase, and know immediately what to do and where to find it, without tracking someone down to ask.

04 · The outcome

Something to react to before signing.

Four concept prototypes gave clients something to react to before signing anything: real screens, realistic synthetic data, and real decisions about what leadership needs to see first. ADU is now teaching around 1,500 employees the same discovery-first thinking, at company scale.
