Agile Defense · Deep dive

A Figma design system that took course production from one module in two weeks to three modules in three days

Senior UX Designer · Agile Defense · 2026

0

Modules in 3 days, up from 1 in 2 weeks

½ day

To set up the design system

0

Days ahead of schedule

Problem

I manage the effort to build a program management training course for government contracts. It has 11 modules of about 10 minutes each, due by the end of 2026. The graphic designer on our team of four drew every graphic for the videos in Adobe Illustrator. Graphics are that designer’s specialty, and one finished module still took two weeks. The team was behind on a strict deadline, with pressure from leadership. With one person on every graphic, progress stopped whenever they got pulled onto something else.

My role

I manage the course effort and review its scripts. I set up the Figma design system with scene templates.

Process

Scripts come first, reviewed by subject matter experts throughout, because the team wasn’t expert in program management. Storyboards follow. Between storyboards and production, AI makes quick sketches of each scene, and the team builds the icons and assets that go into HeyGen for the video.

Decisions

I set up the system in Figma because the team already knew it and liked working in it, so anyone could jump in. It follows atomic design principles and uses Figma components and variables. Each scene template is a visual version of a script section, made mostly of text and icons, so every on-screen asset became a drag-and-drop piece. I kept everything in one shared file, so anyone could see where everyone else was and pick up work as they had time. Setting up the framework took about half a day.

Outcome

After the change, the team finished three modules in three days, up from one module in two weeks, and ended two days ahead of schedule.
