Replacing a one-person Excel sheet with a Jira board the whole Solutions Architect team uses

Senior UX Designer · Agile Defense

0

Pipeline phases on the board

A month

To design and build the board

Every effort

Tracked by the whole team

Problem

The Solutions Architect team works through efforts in phases, and more often than not, someone joins an effort that’s already in progress. The only tracker was an Excel sheet that one person kept. It showed the team and leadership which efforts were in which phase, which were up for recompete, and which upcoming bids the company qualified for. If that person left, the team would be scrambling to find or learn what only they knew. Joining an effort in progress hit three groups: the new solutions architect, the lead onboarding them, and the team or program manager already running the effort.

My role

I designed the board with the lead of the Solutions Architect team. I ran discovery and rounds of interviews with the rest of the team so it fit the whole group.

Process

I started with discovery: interviews with the Solutions Architect team to find the gaps in what they knew about the process. I asked which artifacts each phase needed, whether people knew where to find them, and whether they knew what to do with them once they had them. With the lead, I tracked down the artifacts that already existed and turned them into easier-to-use, prefilled templates, so the work stays consistent and always has what it needs. The lead and I then mapped what each phase looks like, which artifact it needs at which stage, and how to fill it out. Last, I set up the Jira board. Automations create each phase’s subtasks and attach its templates to the tickets, based on which pipeline phase an effort is in. Each phase also links to its OneDrive folder and to a reference for filling out each artifact.

Decisions

I interviewed everyone on the team, because the lead’s sense of what people knew didn’t always match what team members told me. In the interviews they showed me what they didn’t actually know, and we addressed those gaps. I’d have kept the board leaner than it ended up. After the pilot, the team asked for a couple more categories, a timeline view showing who owned what and how many efforts each person was running at once, so future work could be assigned with that in view, and a swim lane that groups tickets by POD to show each business unit. I’d have cut some of that. I conceded because it mattered to the people using the board every day, and the team loved it.

Outcome

The whole Solutions Architect team now uses the board on every effort, and it replaced the Excel sheet. A new solutions architect can join an effort already in progress, at any phase, and know what to do and where to find it, without tracking someone down to ask. The person who used to keep the Excel sheet says the board saves time and is much easier to use and read, and leadership loves it too. The board took a month to design and build. A two-week pilot followed, and it needed only minimal changes afterward.
