01 · The constraint

The tracker depended on one person.

The Solutions Architect team works through efforts in phases, and more often than not, someone joins an effort that’s already in progress.

The only tracker was an Excel sheet that one person kept. If that person left, the team would be scrambling to find or learn what only they knew.

The sheet showed
Which efforts were in which phase, which were up for recompete, and which upcoming bids the company qualified for
Who it affected
The new solutions architect, the lead onboarding them, and the team or program manager already running the effort
02 · My contribution

Start with what people knew.

I designed the board with the VP of Solutions. I ran discovery and rounds of interviews with the rest of the team so it fit the whole group.

How the board came together

  1. InterviewsWhich artifacts does each phase need, can people find them, and do they know what to do with them?
  2. TemplatesWith the VP of Solutions, I tracked down the artifacts that already existed and made them easier-to-use, prefilled templates
  3. Phase mapWe mapped what each phase looks like, which artifact it needs at which stage, and how to fill it out
  4. What I builtThe boardAutomations create each phase’s subtasks and attach its templates

Discovery workshops

Discovery included individual interviews and group workshops. The boards below come from the workshops, which I ran with the Solutions Architect team and the VP of Solutions. Each one is redrawn in a simulated board interface with paraphrased notes, and individual comments are left out.

Participants described strong proposal partnerships and the value of early architect involvement. They also named short-notice requests, trouble finding prior work or specialists, and gaps in customer context. Workarounds included asking colleagues and researching public sources.

The artifacts include outlines, estimates, review slides and visuals. The tasks span research, technical writing, facilitation, specialist coordination and solution design. Both lists are selected examples.

The workshop compared an opportunity review path with direct requests for material. It looked at how skipped steps and missing context affected the handoff, and what it would take to involve architects earlier. Earlier involvement, clearer handoffs and better access to context are ideas the workshop raised.

Process definition

Hard-to-find prior work came up in the first workshop, so I mapped which materials and templates belong in each phase.

Customer context and prior requirements sit in Identify, early outlines and risks in Qualify, design and estimate inputs in Pursue, review materials in Proposal, and debrief and reuse material in Submitted. The artifacts are samples.

03 · The decisions

Let the daily users shape the final board.

Interview everyone, not only the VP of Solutions

WhyThe VP of Solutions’ sense of what people knew didn’t always match what team members told me. In the interviews they showed me what they didn’t actually know, and we addressed those gaps.

Prefill the templates and automate the setup

WhyThe templates keep the work consistent and complete, and the automations mean each phase always has what it needs.

Add the views the team asked for after the pilot

TradeoffI’d have kept the board leaner than it ended up. After the pilot, the team asked for a couple more categories, a timeline view showing who owned what and how many efforts each person was running at once, so future work could be assigned with that in view, and a swim lane that groups tickets by POD to show each business unit. I’d have cut some of that. I conceded because it mattered to the people using the board every day, and the team loved it.

04 · The outcome

The board is now the team's source of truth.

The whole Solutions Architect team now uses the board on every effort, and it replaced the Excel sheet. A new solutions architect can join an effort already in progress, at any phase, and know what to do and where to find it, without tracking someone down to ask.

The person who used to keep the Excel sheet says the board saves time and is much easier to use and read, and leadership loves it too. The board took a month to design and build. A two-week pilot followed, and it needed only minimal changes afterward.
