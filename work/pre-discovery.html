Ten-day Pre-Discovery sprints, and the Fast Plays that start them

Problem

Agile Defense wanted to show a customer how it would solve a real challenge before anything was signed, and before the customer could give access to its users or data. Pre-Discovery was the answer: a 10-day sprint that starts with stakeholder interviews and ends in an interactive prototype that can serve as the basis for the solution if the company wins the work. A win could expand an existing contract or open a new line of business. The sprint needed workshops, and plenty of workshop methods already existed. The team wanted the 20 or so most frequently used ones as quick-hit templates anyone could pick up and run on their own, with a five-minute introduction, even with a prospective customer in the room.

My role

I help run Pre-Discovery sprints with our team of four, and I helped design the process with my team, workshopping it in Mural. I also helped create Fast Plays, our templated workshop methods: the team listed the ones we used most often, and I made Mural templates for them, about 20 in all, and shared them with the company. I run the workshops on our team, so I usually choose the Fast Play that fits what we know about the work. I also contributed to four concept dashboards that came out of these sprints.

Process

Day one covers the workshop, the personas and the journey map. One person runs a stakeholder interview or workshop, usually one of the Discovery Fast Plays, while another fills out the personas and journey map in the background. That checks we’re headed the right way before any prototyping and leaves most of the 10 days for building the concept. Ten days isn’t enough time to reach real end users, so subject matter experts stand in as proxy users. On the readiness dashboard, we also agreed on what “readiness” meant. Then we co-create user stories in a modified Scrum process, planning in Mural. After that, we turn the user stories into sketches and then into an interactive Figma prototype, filled with synthetic data because real data is usually behind access restrictions before a contract. The sprint ends with a walkthrough of the prototype with the customer, through realistic scenarios.

Decisions

In 2024, we set out to template the 20 or so methods we used most often. We kept them internal at first, started with three, and tested them ourselves before sharing more widely. In practice, five to seven get used again and again, and they cover the discovery phase.

Outcome

Pre-Discovery sprints have helped win federal contracts. In one engagement with a prospective client, a four-hour session built on one of the Fast Plays produced persona profiles that guide the client’s UX strategy. The first prototypes were ready in five days and refined over two weeks. The company won that contract and did the work. Four concept dashboards came out of these sprints, each with a prototype the company can build the real solution from if it wins the work. Fast Plays let anyone at the company set up and run a discovery workshop in minutes, even before every stakeholder is known, without deep facilitation experience.
