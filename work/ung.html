Case study · Higher Ed

From 20-plus menus to one a student could use.

I helped decide what stayed, what merged, and what finally got cut on a 5,000+ page university site.

01 · The situation

A site that grew one department at a time.

A public university site grows one department at a time: a page, then a menu, then a sub-menu, and nobody's responsible for the whole thing. By the time I got there, the site had grown past 5,000 pages and 20-plus separate navigation structures, and the admissions section ran through a maze nobody had audited since the university's consolidation, when two institutions were fitted into one site.

02 · The approach

Decide what belongs together.

Redesigning a nav is an information-architecture decision every time: what belongs together, what is load-bearing, what is only old, and what a student can find in two clicks.

Navigation & IA

Helped decide what to keep, merge, or cut across 20-plus navigation menus, and worked on new content templates, landing pages, and custom iconography so visitors could scan the admissions section.

2FA onboarding

Designed the 2FA landing page and instructions that helped over 20,000 students, faculty and staff use the new software, with no spike in support tickets after launch.

03 · The outcome

One structure people could navigate.

The redesign gave the 5,000-plus page site a new structure, and twenty-plus menus became one structure people could navigate. I still think about that project whenever someone asks to add one more link to a navigation bar.

  • UX/UI design led on a 5,000+ page site redesign
  • 20+ navigation menus consolidated into one structure
  • 2FA landing page designed for 20,000+ users
